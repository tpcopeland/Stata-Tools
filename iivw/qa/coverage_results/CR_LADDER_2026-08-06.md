## Which rung the package actually ships

`iivw_fit, vce(fixed)` is **CR1** — `glm [pw=], vce(cluster)` multiplies the
sandwich by `m/(m-1)`, not by CR1S's `m(N-1)/((m-1)(N-p))` and not by nothing.
Measured on the FIPTIW fixture at m=117, N=713, p=2: variance ratio 1.0086209
against 117/116 = 1.0086207, agreement to 2.2e-15. Pinned by
`test_iivw_cr_ladder.do` C4.

This matters because every inflation factor below is quoted against CR0. Had the
baseline been assumed rather than identified, the whole table would have been
quoted against a rung the package does not use.

## What this decides

**§13.2's second branch is the one that fired.** Its own statement of the test
was: if the whole ladder tops out well below the required inflation, the scale
deficit is not outcome-stage sandwich bias and §12's leading hypothesis is dead.

At `n=300` the requirement is **1.1718** and the top of the ladder — CR3, the
most aggressive rung, which approximates the leave-one-cluster-out jackknife —
delivers **1.0880**. On a log scale it closes **53%** of the gap. Coverage moves
0.911 → 0.929, and the Wilson interval [0.911, 0.943] does **not** contain 0.95,
so CR3 + z fails the preregistered `n=300` gate as decisively as the shipped
sandwich does.

The honest reading is narrower than "dead", and worth stating precisely:

- **About half the `n=300` scale deficit is outcome-stage sandwich small-sample
  bias.** That much a leverage-based correction recovers, and CR3 recovering it
  is what §12's hypothesis predicts.
- **The other half is not.** No rung of a family designed specifically to remove
  that bias reaches the required factor. Whatever supplies the remainder is not
  something the outcome-stage sandwich can see.
- Therefore the **stacked two-step influence-function sandwich (§7.1) is now both
  the correct thing to build and the leading remaining explanation to test**, not
  merely the thing owed to Objective A. It is the one candidate on §7's list that
  adds a variance component the CR ladder structurally cannot contain — the
  contribution of the estimated visit-intensity and propensity models.

**No overcoverage appears anywhere on the ladder at z**, at any sample size. This
confirms §4's reading of §5: CR2 + Satterthwaite's 0.985 at `n=600` came from the
**df term**, not from CR2's scale adjustment. The scale axis is well behaved; the
instrument that misbehaved was the one aimed at shape.

## Two observations the external report did not have

**1. The scale deficit does not vanish by `n=600`.** §4's table gives standardized
SD 1.021 at `n=600` and 1.003 at `n=1,200` — i.e. essentially gone. Measured here
against the analytic sandwich the ratio is **1.0682** and **1.0529**: it falls
steeply from 17% to about 7% and then **plateaus near 5%** rather than continuing
to zero.

Two caveats keep this from being a refutation. §4's quantity is the SD of the
pivot studentized by the **refit bootstrap** SE, while this is a ratio of means
against the **CR0 sandwich** — different studentizers and different functionals.
And `COVERAGE_MATRIX_PLAN.md` P1 records refit-bootstrap mean SE / empSD of 1.006
and 0.952 at these two sizes, at **R=200**, where the empSD of a heavy-tailed
estimator is itself noisy. What can be said is that on the analytic sandwich, at
R=1,000, on the gate's own seeds, **the deficit is still ~5% at `n=1,200`** — and
if that survives a like-for-like comparison it weakens the finite-sample framing
of `FIPTIW_NSCALE_2026-07-23.md`.

**2. The CR2/CR3 blow-up is specific to `n=300`.** The 11.5× maximum ratio is not
a property of the method; at `n=600` and `n=1,200` the maxima are 1.72 and 1.36.
Whatever configuration produces an eleven-fold correction is a small-sample
event, not a standing hazard of the estimator. The p95 of max leverage is 0.066
at `n=300` — the typical dataset is not badly conditioned, and a handful are.

## What this does not establish

- **No candidate is cleared.** CR3 + z fails clause 1 of §11 at `n=300`. Clauses
  2–5 were never reached.
- **The FIPTIW default is unchanged** and remains point-only.
- **This is one DGP at one specification**, identity link, `Z` subject-constant.
  §12's scope limits are unchanged, including the open question of whether the
  subject-constant `Z` is what makes this DGP harsher than Coulombe et al.'s own
  validation.
- **The `n=600` and `n=1,200` cells are diagnostic cells**, not gate cells: they
  carry `blk_nsub != 0`, which is exactly the stamp `combine` uses to refuse
  reporting them as a gate verdict.
- Nothing here bears on §7.4's bootstrap-t asymptotic-pivotality claim, which
  remains unverified against a fetched source.

## Reproducing

```bash
# from an isolated scratch copy preserving the repo layout
cd <scratch>/iivw/qa
stata-mp -b do probe_cr_ladder.do run     300 1000 20260715 1 1000
stata-mp -b do probe_cr_ladder.do combine 300 1000 20260715
```

About 10 minutes per cell on this host — the probe runs one weighting pass and
one WLS fit per replication and no bootstrap at all, which is why regenerating
the pool was affordable where re-running the gate is not. The block `.dta` files
in `cr_ladder_2026-08-06/blocks/` are retained but untracked, matching the gate's
convention; unlike the gate's pool they are cheap to regenerate exactly.
